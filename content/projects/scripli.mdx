## Why This Work Mattered

Generative AI broke the implicit trust contract between writers and their readers.

For most of history, if someone submitted an essay, a report, or an article, you could assume a human wrote it.

That assumption no longer holds.

The dominant response has been AI detection — classifiers that try to determine whether text was machine-generated. Detection is the wrong framing. It is probabilistic, produces false positives, and degrades as models improve. A student who wrote their own essay can be flagged. A journalist with a genuine scoop can be accused of fabrication.

Detection treats the writer as a suspect.

The right question is not "did a human write this?" — it is "can this person prove they wrote this?" Proof is binary. Detection is probabilistic.

Scripli is the answer to the second question.

## What I Built

I built Scripli as a complete product, solo, from scratch.

That meant designing and shipping the full system: a Fastify REST API, a Next.js web application, a browser telemetry SDK distributed as both ESM and CJS packages, a Chrome extension for Google Docs, a multi-tenant institutional platform for universities and instructors, an async job pipeline, a certificate issuance and PDF generation system, Stripe billing, and public verification infrastructure.

Every layer of the product reflects a single design constraint: zero document content can ever leave the writer's device.

That constraint shaped every technical decision.

## Systems I Built

### The Writing Session SDK

The core of Scripli is a browser SDK that captures process evidence — not content.

It ships as a standalone JavaScript package (ESM and CJS) and as a React Native module, designed to integrate into any editor environment. It was also wrapped into a Chrome extension that works inside Google Docs.

The SDK records only metadata: timing patterns, revision behavior, session events, and client-side document state signals. No text is ever transmitted. A technically sophisticated user can confirm this independently.

That meant I had to solve for a hard constraint on day one: the SDK needed to be meaningful enough to distinguish real human sessions from automated or copy-pasted submissions, without ever touching the words.

I built the SDK to be embeddable anywhere: the Scripli editor, third-party applications via the public package, the Chrome extension, and eventually Word and LMS-embedded editors.

### Human Authenticity Certificates

After a writing session closes, Scripli analyzes the session evidence and issues a Human Authenticity Certificate (HAC) — a cryptographically signed, tamper-evident record that the session occurred.

I built the full certificate pipeline: session analysis, certificate issuance, cryptographic signing, PDF generation, and public verification.

Each certificate has a short identifier that maps to a public verification page. Anyone — a professor, an editor, a hiring manager — can verify a certificate without creating an account or treating Scripli as a trusted intermediary.

The certificate also supports optional Bitcoin timestamp anchoring through OpenTimestamps, which allows the existence of a session to be proved against a public, immutable record independent of the Scripli platform.

Revocation is supported as a first-class feature. Certificates can be revoked with a visible reason, and verification pages show revocation status so third parties always see the current state.

### Institutional Platform

Individual writers are one side of the market. The larger opportunity is institutions.

I built a full multi-tenant platform for universities, companies, and educational providers. Every resource is scoped to an institution from day one: users, documents, sessions, certificates, courses, and assignments.

Instructors can create courses, assign writing sessions to students, and view class-wide analysis. Students can see their own writing history, manage documents, and download their certificates.

I also built a defense request workflow for cases where a student has been accused of AI-generated writing. The student can submit their writing evidence through the platform as part of a formal appeal, with the session record and certificate supporting their case.

### LMS Integration

Institutions already have learning management systems.

I built LTI 1.3 support so Scripli can be launched directly from Canvas, Moodle, or any standards-compliant LMS. An instructor creates an assignment inside their LMS, students click through and land in a Scripli session already scoped to that assignment. The session record is linked back to the original assignment context.

That mattered because adoption inside institutions depends on reducing friction for instructors. Requiring every instructor to log into a separate platform to manage assignments was a non-starter. The LTI path removes that barrier.

### Writing Fingerprint and Wrapped

Beyond certificates, I built a writing fingerprint view that gives writers a behavioral profile of their writing patterns across sessions — not a score to be gamed, but a personal record of how they actually write.

I also built Wrapped: an annual writing year-in-review that summarizes a writer's year of sessions, behavioral patterns, and certificate history. It generates a shareable card and a personal summary page.

### Billing and Platform Operations

Scripli runs as a live commercial product.

I integrated Stripe for subscription billing with a founding member tier, webhook handling for subscription lifecycle events, and a billing dashboard inside the web app.

I also built the full operational infrastructure: async job queue for certificate issuance, data retention policies configurable per institution, weekly digest emails for instructors, demo environment cleanup, and a super-admin analytics dashboard for platform-level visibility.

## Engineering Decisions

### Privacy as Architecture, Not Policy

Most platforms make privacy a policy choice. A policy can change. An architecture cannot.

I designed Scripli so that no document text can be transmitted or stored — not because of a rule, but because the system provides no path to do so. The client computes one-way representations of document state and sends only those.

This constraint eliminated a large class of simpler implementations and raised the difficulty of certain analyses. I chose the constraint anyway because the platform's credibility depends entirely on that guarantee being independently verifiable, not just stated.

A privacy policy is not evidence. An architecture is.

### Public Verification Without a Trusted Intermediary

Anyone can check a Scripli certificate.

That design decision — verification without an account, without trusting the platform — was deliberate. The goal was to make the certificate meaningful regardless of whether Scripli exists in five years.

A certificate that requires a platform to stay online to be checkable is not a certificate. It is a lookup table with a vendor dependency.

The verification path is intentionally minimal and designed to be cacheable, so the certificate remains checkable even under adverse conditions.

### Building for Both Ends of the Market

Individual writers need a simple, fast path: write, get a certificate, share it.

Institutions need enrollment management, assignment workflows, class-wide analysis, audit trails, LMS integration, and billing.

These are very different products sharing the same core data model.

I designed the multi-tenant architecture from the beginning with this in mind, so the same session records and certificates that work for an individual writer also feed into instructor dashboards, course-level analysis, and institution-wide reporting without a separate data pipeline.

## What Building This Taught Me

### Proof Is Harder Than Detection

Every AI detection product has the same architecture: input text, output probability.

Proof is a harder problem because it requires you to build infrastructure around the absence of something — the content — rather than its presence.

You cannot analyze what you do not have. That meant building a different kind of signal, one that is meaningful without revealing the document.

Getting that right required thinking carefully about what actually distinguishes a human writing session from an automated one, and making sure the answer was legible to a third party who was not in the room.

### Trust Is Infrastructure

Scripli is not primarily a writing tool.

It is trust infrastructure for a world where the default assumption about text has changed.

The certificate is the product. But what makes the certificate valuable is not the platform — it is that the platform can be verified independently, that the signatures can be checked, that the privacy guarantee can be confirmed.

Building for trust means every layer of the system has to be defensible.

Not just secure. Defensible.

A user should be able to understand why their data is safe — not just be told it is.

### Friction Is a Design Failure

The hardest part of building Scripli was not the cryptography, the SDK, or the multi-tenancy.

It was making the path from "I need to prove I wrote this" to "I have a certificate I can share" as short as possible.

Every extra step is a student who gives up. Every extra login prompt is an instructor who does not assign it.

Adoption inside institutions depends on the product being genuinely easier than the alternative, not just technically better.

That meant building the LTI integration, the Chrome extension, the shareable verification link, and the defense request workflow — all paths designed to reduce the distance between need and proof.

## How I Think

The right framing for Scripli is not "AI detection, but better."

It is "authorship infrastructure for a world that has lost its default assumptions."

That reframing changes what you build.

Detection asks: is this AI?

Proof asks: can you show that it was not?

The answer to the second question requires building a system that records trustworthy evidence, seals it cryptographically, makes it publicly verifiable, and does none of that at the cost of the writer's privacy.

That is what Scripli is.
